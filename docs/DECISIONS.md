# Settled decisions

Every ruling that shapes a learnable frequency list, dated, with its rationale and where it is enforced. A
ruling is the developer's unless marked otherwise. Sources: the build records (`full/<lang>/junk-review.md`,
`full/<lang>/steps.md`, `full/<lang>/README.md` on the `full-*` branches), Honeyeater's decision log
(`docs/design/02-decision-log.md`, the 2026-11-01 amendments) and honeyeater-lemmatiser's `decisions/`.

A new ruling goes here, in the language's `junk-review.md`, and into any rules file it changes
(`scripts/review/prompts/`), in the same commit as the change it causes. Where code and a ruling disagree, the
ruling wins and the code is a bug. The procedure that applies these is [PLAYBOOK.md](PLAYBOOK.md).

## What the list is

| date | ruling | why | enforced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-01 | **The loaded list is the learnable set.** A lemma is learnable by default exactly when the language's loaded list ranks it; anything else starts ignored (`off_list`). No separate depth band: the per-language default-learn rank (es 25,000) drafted for this was dropped. | One source of truth for what a learner learns; a word in no list is one the learner never meets by default. | Honeyeater `lexicon.lemma_defaults.classify`, `_off_list` (decision log, 2026-11-01 sixth amendment to D11/D12; on PR #766, open at the time of writing); here, everything that decides what is on the list |
| 2026-11-01, amended 2026-11-02 | **Cutoff per language, chosen by the developer**: review to 30k, then cut. Chosen from **word samples per band** (glossed samples, the words just above 30k, what a deeper cut adds) and a **cross-language comparison at the same rank**, not from the real-word percentage (less relevant because the list is filtered anyway). es 30k, ja 30k, de pending. | The list's own cut replaces an arbitrary depth; words show what a cut keeps. | `language.top_n` and `language.cutoff` in `corpora/<lang>.yaml`; `scripts/review/band_samples.py` (`full/<code>/band-samples.md`); `frequer pipeline` waits for `cutoff` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Reading levels count only learnable lemmas.** | A level estimate should reflect the words a learner is asked to learn. | Honeyeater `nlp.text_statistics.learnable_lemma_ids` (2026-11-01 eighth amendment, D11/D12 + D25; PR #767, merges after #766) |
| 2026-11-01 | **Course completion ignores off-list words**: a lesson's auto-completion asks only its learnable (listed) words, minus those the learner ignored; an off-list word never blocks a unit. **An empty lesson never auto-completes.** | A course is complete when its learnable words are known. | Honeyeater `srs.course_completion` (sixth amendment; PR #766) |
| 2026-11-01 | **Phrase mining breaks at any lemma ignored by default**, replacing its proper-noun test. | A phrase built on a word the learner never meets by default is not a phrase to teach. | Honeyeater `nlp.phrase_corpus` (sixth amendment; PR #766) |
| 2026-11-01 | **Combined deploy**: a change to the app's default rules ships in the same deploy as the new lists (or back to back). | #766 removes the proper-noun default rule; ahead of the cleaned snapshots it would make every name in the old 20k lists learnable. | Stated in PR #766's body ("Merge timing") and its session report (`docs/plans/completed-plans/learnable-names-and-rank-depth.md` §Merge timing), not in the decision log; Honeyeater `docs/runbook/language-pipeline.md` |

## Junk heuristics

| date | ruling | why | enforced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-01 | **2+ flags drop; 1 flag goes to review** within the review window (with `review_multi_flag`, 2+ flags in the window are reviewed too); proper-noun flags always go to review. | Single flags are often real words; correlated flags on English acronyms are not independent evidence. | `frequer.junk.route` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Diversity is a flag only, never a reordering**: the ranking stays raw frequency. Thresholds per language: es 0.6, de 0.3, ja 0.2 (calibrated on samples; HPLT documents are long). | Low diversity marks templates and spam, but reranking would distort real words. | `JunkConfig.diversity_max_clumping`; `frequer.junk.compute_flags` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Accent merges**: an unaccented spelling merges into the lemma owning the accented spelling at 5x, only with a unique owner, never when a review says `keep` (two words: *secretaria*/*secretaría*). **ñ is a letter** (es); **ß is a letter** (de, except the `ss` transliteration); German uses umlaut letter maps; a dominant plain spelling (*bewusst* over *bewußt*) never merges. | Web text drops accents; the lemmatiser files the bare forms under non-words. | `frequer.junk.accent_merges`, `folds`; `JunkConfig.accent_ratio`, `fold_maps` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Proper-noun evidence per language**: es capitalisation in running text (drop >= 90 %, keep <= 20 %, review between); de article/adjective usage, every PROPN reviewed (case says nothing); ja Sudachi 固有名詞 subtags (人名/組織名 drop only with the tagger's PROPN; 地名 pending names; others reviewed). | Each script gives different evidence; a tagger's PROPN alone is not a drop. | `propn_band` (`propn_evidence: case/dictionary`), `propn_usage`; `frequer/propn.py`, `propn_dict.py`, `propn_usage.py` |
| 2026-11-01 | **A reviewed `keep` outranks the automatic proper-noun drop**; a reviewed decision outranks routing; a merge target counts as reviewed. | Every automated drop must be reversible by review (famous institutions, era names). | `frequer.junk.route` |
| 2026-11-01 | **New rules are validated on samples before they act**, and automated only once proven on reviewed data. | A rule that over-flags silently removes real words (the rejected other-language rule; ja diversity 0.2). | Practice; PLAYBOOK §4 |

## Review protocol

| date | ruling | why | enforced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-01 | **Two independent reviewers (Sonnet) plus an adjudicator (Opus)** per lemma, with example sentences; reviewers never see each other's answers. `# question` lines go to the developer, never settled silently. The coordinator checks agreed merges and drops before adjudication. | Judgement with oversight; agreement measures how clear the rules are. | `scripts/review/review.py` (two stages), prompts in `scripts/review/prompts/` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Unflagged lemmas are triaged**: two screeners, the union of suspects through the full protocol; the release's own entries (units, homograph keys, learnable names) are not candidates. | A blind sample found a few per cent junk among unflagged keeps. | `scripts/review/screen.py`; `common.release_entry` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Places are `pending-names` in review**, decided only by the learnable-names list. | Place judgements are made once, consistently, in Honeyeater's reviewed list. | `junk.route` (`pending-names`, `place-drop` with `names_final`) |
| 2026-11-01 | **Every decision lives in `corpora/review/<lang>.tsv`**; scripts apply rounds append-only, a re-decision under a new ruling keeps the old decision in the reason. | Auditable and reversible. | `scripts/review/collect.py`, `common.apply_round`; `frequer.junk.load_review` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Spot audits** (random keeps and drops per band) estimate, never decide. | The developer reads real-lemma estimates per band. | `scripts/review/audit.py`, `report.py`; `collect.py` refuses audit rounds |

## What is on the list

| date | ruling | why | enforced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-01 | **Learnable place names**: countries, continents, oceans, well-known cities (especially ones named differently in the target language; more willing for the target language's own countries), major regions and provinces learners need; **every US state with a target-language name**; first-level divisions of target-language countries **only if famous**; capitals of smaller countries only when well known. **Same-spelled foreign places are ignored** (an exact-spelling test: *Múnich*, *París* differ). **Bare forms** texts use are added (*Cuzco*, *Andes*, 北京, 大阪). Language names stay learnable. | Names a learner is genuinely likely to need, not any large city anywhere. | Honeyeater `reference/learnable-names/<lang>.json` (seventh amendment; `scripts/build_learnable_names.py`; es 1,010, de 749, ja 1,224 at `1bf77f0`); here `names_final`, `append_learnable_names` (`frequer/names.py`: every listed name gets an entry, see the 2026-11-02 ruling below) |
| 2026-11-02 | **Every learnable name gets an entry, even uncounted**: counted names below the cut are appended in rank order; a name the release files under another lemma is listed with that lemma, not appended; an uncounted single-token name is appended bare (frequency 0); a multiword name the release doesn't emit as a unit is not appended and goes to `full/<code>/learnable-multiword-names.tsv` as input for lemma release 1.6's unit training. | The loaded list is the learnable set, so a learnable name must be on it; a key the release never produces never matches. | `frequer/names.py` (`plan`), `frequer/build.py` with `append_learnable_names` |
| 2026-11-02 | **de: 12 missing places added to the learnable list** (Atlantik, Pazifik, Arktis, Sibirien, Sahara, Skandinavien, Korea, Weißrussland, Mazedonien, Westdeutschland, Dublin, Seoul). | Gaps found in review. | Honeyeater `reference/learnable-names/de.json` |
| 2026-11-01 | **There is no "never override a common word" rule**: learnable status is a union of the frequency list and the place list, so a listed place that is also a common word is kept, and a name that is also a common word is judged as a place. | The two lists answer different questions. | `junk.route` (listed → `review-keep`); the build appends listed names below the cut |
| 2026-11-01 | **Famous institutions are kept** (nationally famous universities, ministries, broadcasters, public bodies; major international bodies); **other names drop**: people, minor companies, brands, products, websites, events, titles. de 2026-11-02: all ten borderline institutions kept (DB, Telekom, WDR, Greenpeace, UEFA, Hamas, taz, NABU, KfW, EKD). | Learners meet famous institutions by name in the news. | Review rules (`prompts/<lang>/rules-*.md`, `institutions-*.md`); es: 158 kept of 754 screened picks |
| 2026-11-01 | **Numbers: a curated generative set per language** — 0-30, ordinals 1-30, the non-inferable tens and hundreds, scales up to trillions, irregular and sound-change forms (三百, 六百, 八百). Any other spelled number built only from number words is ignored, however frequent; inferable compounds (ja 二十) are left out. | A learner should be able to work out any number without learning each as a lemma. | Honeyeater `lexicon.number_words.number_is_learnable` and `reference/number-words/<code>.json` (schema v3; sixth amendment); here `number_rule` (`frequer.numbers.app_number_verdicts` asks the reference checkout; `junk.route` `number-drop`) |
| 2026-11-01 | **English leakage excluded; real loans a learner wants are kept** (es *show*, *web*, *online*; de *Computer*, *Team*). A non-target token whose real-word reading is a minority drops. | Leakage is web noise; loans are vocabulary. | `corpora/exclude/<lang>.tsv` (`keep-*` rows protect loans), `foreign` flag, review rules |
| 2026-11-01 | **Abbreviations excluded for now**, to be lemmatised to their full words later; de 2026-11-02: everyday abbreviations too (zB, Hbf, cm, mg). | An abbreviation is not a word to learn on its own. | Exclusion list; review rules (`merge:` into the full word when obvious) |
| 2026-11-02 | **de: hyphenated compounds are judged on the ordinary rules**, no special class; **superlative-only stems are kept**. | No separate class is needed. | de review rules |
| 2026-11-02 | **de: ordinals above 30 are ignored** (the number rule returns False for them; Honeyeater #766). | The curated number set covers ordinals 1-30. | Honeyeater `lexicon.number_words.number_is_learnable` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Acronyms used as words are kept** (IVA, ADN, PDF, USB; de EU, GmbH, WLAN); brand, company and specialist acronyms drop. | They are vocabulary. | Review rules |
| 2026-11-01 | **Brands drop**; a genericised brand stays only where the language uses it as the ordinary word (es: where the DLE accepts it, *polaroid*). | Brands are names. | Review rules |
| 2026-11-01 | **Lemmatiser fragments are merged, not dropped** (*jugabl*, 送 → 送る, *Famili* → *Familie*). | The counts belong to a real word. | `review_multi_flag`; `merge:` decisions; `junk.apply_merges` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Adult vocabulary**: common words a learner meets in ordinary reading are kept (風俗); spam jargon is dropped (デリヘル, セフレ). | Honest coverage without spam. | Review rules |
| 2026-11-01 | **Era names are kept** (令和, 平成, 昭和). | Learners meet them in dates. | ja review rules; a reviewed keep outranks the dictionary drop |
| 2026-11-02 | **es split loans**: a two-token loan whose halves only occur together is one entry; the first half merges into the compound, the second half drops (*ping* → *ping-pong*; *hip hop*, *modus operandi*). | Merging both would count each occurrence twice. | `corpora/review/es.tsv` (`merge:`/`drop`) |
| 2026-11-02 | **es *halar*/*hala***: the broken lemma is split by form; *Hala* forms go to *hala* (the interjection), the rest merge into *halo*. | One key held two words. | `split:` decisions, `frequer.junk.apply_splits` |
| 2026-11-02 | **Interjection keys**: an interjection gets its own key when it is used often and its meaning has drifted or isn't obvious without learning it (*venga*, *anda*). The list cannot create a key the release never produces. | A learner must learn such an interjection as its own word. | Lemmatiser work: Honeyeater `docs/plans/lemmatiser-release-1.6.md` §5 |
| 2026-11-02 | **es interjection verb forms are judged by learner need**: where the release keys the interjection as its own lemma (*vaya*, *ojalá*, *hala*) it stays; where it folds it into the verb (*venga* → *venir*, *vamos* → *ir*) it counts inside the verb's entry. | A key the release never produces never matches a token in the reader. | Review decisions; full/es/junk-review.md |
| 2026-11-01 | **Homograph keys for place/word pairs** (a place that is also a common word gets its own key) are planned for lemma release 1.6. | Today one key carries both senses, so the union rule keeps both. | Honeyeater `docs/plans/lemmatiser-release-1.6.md` (not yet implemented) |
| 2026-11-07 | **zh: names are learnable things** (lemmatiser ruling R12, developer 2026-11-05): places (all prefecture-level Chinese cities; counties and districts only if nationally famous), holidays, dynasties, everyday platforms and products, famous institutions, newspapers and household-name companies, named diseases, ethnic groups, planets, canonical works (四大名著, 论语) stay; people drop. Places are decided in review (no `reference/learnable-names/zh.json` yet); the kept names are exported to `full/zh/learnable-names.tsv` as its input. | R12; the place, institution and brand rulings, by analogy (coordinator calls 1-4, 16, 17). | `scripts/review/prompts/zh/rules-*.md`; `corpora/review/zh.tsv`; `full/zh/junk-review.md` |
| 2026-11-07 | **zh: deities and objects of worship keep as religious vocabulary** (上帝, 真主, 佛祖, 观音, 耶稣, 玉帝, 女娲); **people, historical or legendary, drop** (孔子, 毛泽东, 习近平, 炎帝); a name that became an everyday term keeps (雷锋 in 学雷锋). | The people ruling; a god's name is religious vocabulary a reader meets, not a person to look up (coordinator calls 4, 14, 25). | zh rules §Calls 4, 14, 25 |
| 2026-11-07 | **zh: the release's word cut stands**: a multi-character word the segmenter made is a word of the list even when compositional (户外广告); only a mis-cut drops or merges: a string crossing a word boundary (书目的), or a fusion the lemmatiser guide splits (verb + 着/出, 未 + verb, degree adverb + adjective, verb + free object). ## Lemmatiser releases (honeyeater-lemmatiser)

| date | ruling | why | enforced |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | **The guide is binding**; a convention change is a ruling in `decisions/` plus a new guide version and a relabel, in one commit. **Labels come from annotators, not tools.** | Gold must measure, not echo a tool. | honeyeater-lemmatiser `CLAUDE.md`, `guides/GUIDE.md` |
| 2026-10-24 | **Gold: three annotators plus an adjudicator; training data: one annotator with sampled oversight** (a blind judge; a batch above 1.5 % judged lemma error is re-annotated). | Gold is the measuring stick; label noise of 0.1-0.2 % caps accuracy by about that much, and unseen forms dominate errors. | `decisions/labelling-protocol-2026-10-24.md` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Accent- and umlaut-stripped training copies**: training passes may add copies of annotated sentences with diacritics removed, labels unchanged (es á é í ó ú ü → a e i o u, **ñ stays**; de ä ö ü → a o u, **ß stays**; ja n/a). Never gold or held-out; measured both ways; pinned only if normal-text accuracy does not drop; copies at most ~20 % of training examples. | ~1,200 unaccented forms in the es 50k build were filed under non-words. | `decisions/training-accent-stripped-copies-2026-11-01.md`; implemented after 1.5.x (no release so far trains on copies): `model/accents.py`, `corpus.py --accent-copies`, `evaluate.py --strip-accents`, the `release.py` gate |
| 2026-11-01 | **Release gates**: new lemma and sentence accuracy >= the pinned release's on gold and on held-out (tolerance 0 unless the developer rules otherwise; ja 1.5.0's near-tie was accepted as passing); parity with the app runtime's lemma step: **0 differing sentences**; packaging reproducible (built twice, one sha256, from a committed tree). | The release must behave in the app exactly as it scored, and be rebuildable byte for byte. | honeyeater-lemmatiser `model/release.py` (gate), `model/lemma_parity.py`, `model/package.py`; [RELEASE-PLAYBOOK](https://github.com/comprehension-compass/honeyeater-lemmatiser/blob/main/docs/RELEASE-PLAYBOOK.md) |
| 2026-10-31 | **Units shipping bar**: >= 95 % precision and >= 80 % recall per type on held-out; an off-list source is switched on per language only at >= 90 % precision on dev and held-out. | Units change what the reader shows. | honeyeater-lemmatiser `model/units/README.md`; `export.py --open` |
| 2026-11-01 | **Figures are lemmatised as the app spells them** (v3.36, v3.37: numbers, dates, ordinals, percentages, times); those relabels were written by a script calling the app's speller, a ruling-sanctioned exception to "labels come from annotators". | Lemma identity must match the runtime's number speller. | honeyeater-lemmatiser `decisions/rulings-v3.36.md`, `v3.37.md`; `check.py`, `corpus.py`, `evaluate.py` |
| open | **Not yet ruled**: whose predicted lemmas train the unit component (1.4.1/1.5.0 used 1.3.0's; `release.py --unit-lemmas` keeps that); whether accent-stripped copies also feed the lookup list (kept model-only); whether to leave out ambiguous accent pairs (*está*/*esta*). | — | To the developer |
