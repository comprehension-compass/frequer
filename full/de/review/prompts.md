# Review prompts (de, 2026-11-02)

Each batch went to two independent reviewer agents (Sonnet), then the disagreements to an adjudicator (Opus).
The rules are `rules.md`. The batch files (`batchNN.md`: lemma, rank, frequency, POS, flags and up to three
example sentences) are regenerated by `scripts/batches.py` from `items.tsv` and the examples that
`scripts/examples.py` streams from the counted sources. They are not committed because the example sentences are
web text.

## Reviewer (A and B; B's prompt names A's directory as off-limits instead)

> You are an independent reviewer for a German learner frequency list. Work alone: use only the two files named
> below. Do not open any other file or directory (in particular nothing under review-r1/B/ or other reviewers'
> outputs), and do not use any dictionary, web lookup or tool other than reading these files and writing your
> output.
> 1. Read the binding rules: `<dir>/rules.md`
> 2. Read your batch: `<dir>/batchNN.md` (each `## <lemma>` entry has rank, frequency, POS, the flag(s) and up to
>    3 example sentences).
> 3. Decide every lemma, in order, using your own knowledge of German and the examples.
> 4. Write `<dir>/A/batchNN.tsv` with exactly one line per lemma, tab-separated: `<lemma exactly as in the ##
>    heading>\t<decision>\t<short reason>`, where decision is `keep`, `drop` or `merge:<lemma>`. Put any
>    `# question <lemma>: <what is unclear>` lines at the end of the file. Do not skip lemmas.
>
> Reply with just: the number of decision lines written and counts of keep/drop/merge.

## Adjudicator

> You are the adjudicator for a German learner frequency list review. Two independent reviewers disagreed on the
> lemmas in your file. Use only these two files and your own knowledge of German (no other files, no web lookups):
> 1. Binding rules: `<dir>/rules.md`
> 2. Disagreements: `<dir>/adjudicate/adjNN.md` (each `## <lemma>` has rank, frequency, POS, flags, both reviewers'
>    decisions with reasons, and example sentences).
>
> For each lemma, decide by the rules: `keep`, `drop` or `merge:<lemma>` (German spelling, nouns capitalised).
> Write `<dir>/adjudicate/adjNN.tsv`, one line per lemma, `<lemma>\t<decision>\t<short reason>`, in order, none
> skipped. Add `# question <lemma>: <what is unclear>` lines at the end for anything the rules do not settle.

## Pipeline

1. `scripts/junkrun.py out.tsv`: merge the counts once (cached pickle), run frequer's filters (exclusions, merges,
   junk flags, routing with the current review file), write every ranked lemma's flags and action.
2. Pick the pending lemmas in the window, minus learnable place names (Honeyeater `reference/learnable-names/de.json`:
   decided `keep` automatically): `items.json`.
3. `scripts/examples.py wanted.json examples.json 60000000`: stream Tatoeba and 7 HPLT files, keep up to 3
   sentences per lemma (distinct documents) matching its commonest surface forms.
4. `scripts/batches.py <dir> 300 r1`: batch files; launch the reviewers; `scripts/compare.py <dir> r1 150`:
   agreed decisions and adjudication files; launch the adjudicators.
5. `scripts/assemble.py <dir> r1[,r2...]`: write `corpora/review/de.tsv` (every decision) and `corpora/exclude/de.tsv`
   (the foreign-flagged decisions: drop -> exclude-english, keep -> keep-loan). Number-rule drops are added
   from Honeyeater's `number_is_learnable` (see `../steps.md`).
6. Rerun step 1 with the review file: drops pull new lemmas into the window; review them the same way (round 2).
